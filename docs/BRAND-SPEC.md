# Zoe Brand Spec

This document is the working source of truth for Zoe brand direction across marketing pages, journeys, imagery, UI styling, and copy.

For the official agent-facing implementation handoff, also see [docs/ZOE-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md](/Users/tony/Documents/zoe-landing/docs/ZOE-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md).

## 1. Brand Summary

**One-line brand definition**

Zoe is a quiet, spiritually grounded companion that helps people walk with Jesus throughout the ordinary hours of the day.

**Core promise**

Zoe helps people move from fragmented good intentions to an integrated life with God.

**What the brand should feel like**

- Calm, not cold
- Hopeful, not shiny
- Premium, not luxurious-for-show
- Spiritually serious, not heavy-handed
- Human, not AI-first

## 2. Brand Positioning

### What Zoe is

- A day-long discipleship companion
- An SMS-first experience with almost no learning curve
- A tool that helps people pay attention to God, Scripture, and real life
- A product that points outward to church, community, and faithful action

### What Zoe is not

- Not an AI pastor
- Not a replacement for the Holy Spirit
- Not a substitute for church, pastoral care, or Christian community
- Not a generic chatbot with Bible verses
- Not a productivity app wearing spiritual language

### Parent brand context

Freedomology is the parent company. Zoe is its faith and discipleship product expression. When the two appear together, Zoe should still feel like the primary user-facing identity on consumer surfaces.

## 3. Emotional Outcome

Every Zoe touchpoint should leave people feeling:

- More grounded
- More attentive
- More hopeful
- Less fragmented
- More willing to take a faithful next step

The tone should never make users feel managed, optimized, or spiritually marketed to.

## 4. Messaging Pillars

### Day-long devotion

The central Zoe idea is not “do a devotional.” It is “walk with Jesus all day long.”

### Quiet companionship

Zoe should feel like a calm presence, not an interruption. The product joins the rhythm of a person’s day instead of demanding a new system.

### Private by default

Trust is a brand pillar, not a footnote. Privacy, consent, and clear boundaries should stay visible in both copy and design.

### No app required

Zoe lives in text messages. This is a strategic part of the brand because it makes the experience feel immediate, lightweight, and human-scale.

### Points outward

Zoe exists to help people engage Scripture more deeply and show up to church and community more fully. It should never sound self-enclosed or dependency-forming.

## 5. Voice & Copy Style

### Voice attributes

- Quiet
- Direct
- Warm
- Literate
- Pastoral without being preachy
- Confident without sounding salesy

### Copy rules

- Prefer short declarative lines over inflated marketing phrasing
- Use concrete spiritual language instead of abstract self-help language
- Make room for reflection; do not over-explain every idea
- Write like a thoughtful guide, not a hype brand
- Let the strongest claims come from clarity, not intensity

### Preferred language

- Walk with Jesus
- Day-long devotion
- Pay attention
- Remember what matters
- A quiet tool
- Private by default
- No app required
- A companion for the space between Sundays

### Avoid language like

- Revolutionary
- Optimize your spiritual life
- AI pastor
- Faith operating system
- Hyper-personalized engagement engine
- Frictionless discipleship platform
- Productivity-first spiritual framing

### Theological posture

Brand voice should reflect humility and doctrinal restraint. Where theology is debated, Zoe should sound careful and grounded rather than overly certain for the sake of conversion copy.

## 6. Visual System — Canonical Zoe

If no variant is specified, use this as the primary Zoe look.

### Canonical reference

The current source of truth for Zoe's visual system is the Stitch exploration screen below:

- Project: `Zoe Brand Lab` (`projects/5459022810738497776`)
- Screen: `Zoe Landing - Modern Desktop` (`9303d5e7daa84cedb0a697744c80bcd1`)

This screen is the benchmark for future landing pages, supporting pages, and design-system decisions. If new work drifts away from this reference, bring it back toward this screen rather than inventing a new direction.

### Mood

- Light
- Warm
- Calm
- Trustworthy
- Modern
- Premium without showiness
- Casual and disarming

### Visual hallmarks

- Light-only layouts with no dark dramatic sections
- Warm oat and beige canvases instead of stark white
- Inter-dominant typography with only a tiny serif trust accent
- Bright jade used for action and emphasis, never muddy emerald
- Balanced, symmetrical, precise spacing with strong restraint
- Product clarity first, styled feeling second

### Core palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oat Canvas | `#FCF9F4` | Default page background |
| Soft Oat | `#F6F3EE` | Secondary sections and tonal grouping |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, overlays, and elevated surfaces |
| Soft Charcoal | `#1C1C19` | Primary text and high-contrast copy |
| Primary Jade | `#00C292` | Main CTA and primary accent |
| Deep Jade | `#004935` | On-jade text and disciplined deep contrast |
| Quiet Outline | `#BBCAC1` | Optional subtle strokes when needed |

### Color behavior

- Jade is the only primary accent color for the canonical Zoe system
- Primary CTAs should feel bright, fresh, and growth-oriented
- Avoid pine, forest, muddy emerald, or any green that reads as bank, university, or institution
- Avoid dark sections as a default brand move
- Avoid relying on orange, brown, or gold as visible secondary brand accents on core pages
- Use tonal surface shifts instead of bold contrast blocks to create hierarchy

### Typography

- **Primary system:** `Inter`
- **Optional accent serif:** `Newsreader`

**Headline behavior**

- Main hero headlines should stay sans-serif
- Section headlines should generally stay sans-serif
- Keep tracking slightly tight and the overall hierarchy calm and confident
- The page should feel more like premium modern product design than an editorial layout

**Serif behavior**

- Serif is optional, not required
- If serif appears, use it as a tiny trust-bearing accent only
- Limit serif usage to at most one controlled moment per screen
- Good uses: a short eyebrow, citation, trust note, or reflective aside
- Do not use serif for nav, buttons, long body copy, feature grids, or the main hero

**Body behavior**

- Use sans-serif for UI, body copy, forms, metadata, and support text
- Maintain high legibility and a restrained tone
- Labels can be uppercase if tracking is generous and the use is disciplined

### Composition & spacing rules

- Prefer centered or carefully balanced layouts over magazine-style asymmetry
- Use generous whitespace to create calm rather than drama
- Section spacing should usually land in the `64px` to `80px` range
- Component spacing should usually land in the `24px` to `32px` range
- Smaller internal grouping should usually land in the `12px` to `16px` range
- If a page starts feeling editorial, reduce stylistic flourishes before reducing space

### Shape & surface rules

- Rounded corners should feel soft and premium, not playful
- Cards should rely on tonal layering rather than strong borders
- Visible dividers should be avoided where spacing or surface contrast can do the job
- Shadows should be extremely subtle or absent
- Buttons should feel flat, bright, and confident, not glossy or dramatic

### No longer core default behavior

These may still appear in controlled variants, but they are no longer the default Zoe system:

- Dark contemplative sections
- Cyan-forward accents and glow effects
- Painterly scenic illustration as the default homepage language
- Serif-led hero typography
- Editorial asymmetry or magazine-like staggering

## 7. Imagery Direction

### Default Zoe imagery

- Product-first and layout-first rather than image-first
- Light, minimal, grounded imagery when imagery is needed
- Warm, candid, human photography over scenic or symbolic illustration
- Simple supporting visuals that reinforce clarity and trust
- Spiritual depth communicated through restraint, copy, and pacing rather than overt metaphor

### Photography guidance

Use photography when it feels grounded, human, warm, and real. Good Zoe photography feels candid, reflective, and lived-in without becoming moody or self-consciously editorial. It should not feel like a startup team shoot, worship-conference promo, or smiling call-center ad.

### Do not do

- Generic SaaS dashboards as hero imagery
- Painterly sky-and-hills scenes as the default homepage language
- Purple-heavy palettes unless explicitly requested
- Loud futurist AI aesthetics
- Plastic-looking AI faces
- Busy collage or scrapbook treatments on core Zoe pages
- AI-generated versions of the Zoe wordmark

## 8. Motion & Interaction

- Favor stillness over spectacle
- Motion should feel atmospheric, slow, and depth-driven
- Use subtle parallax, fade, and drift when it adds meaning
- Avoid bouncy startup motion, flashy counters, or aggressive hover theatrics
- The experience should feel calm on mobile first

## 9. Product UX Principles

- Mobile-first is the default posture
- Narrative scroll is often stronger than dense multi-column marketing layouts
- CTA moments should feel invitational, not pushy
- Trust and privacy language should be concrete and visible
- Deep-link experiences from SMS should feel lightweight and uncluttered

## 10. Copy Do / Don’t

### Do

- Sound like a wise, grounded guide
- Use spiritual language with sincerity and restraint
- Emphasize companionship, remembrance, reflection, and next steps
- Point people back to God, Scripture, and community

### Don’t

- Lead with the AI
- Sound like a growth hack
- Overuse exclamation points, emojis, or trendy internet tone
- Promise certainty where the product should be humble
- Imply Zoe replaces pastors, friends, church, or prayer itself

## 11. Sanctioned Variants

Variants are contextual expressions of Zoe, not separate brands.

### Variant A — Default Zoe

Use for the main `zoe.live` experience, the primary landing page, broad consumer-facing surfaces, and any context where Zoe should feel most recognizably itself.

**Direction**

- Warm oat backgrounds and light-only layouts
- Inter-led typography with only a tiny serif accent when needed
- Bright jade CTAs with no dark green gradient logic
- Balanced spacing, restrained cards, and premium product clarity
- Simple, calm, human trust signal rather than atmospheric mood-building

**Feels like**

A lightweight, premium product that feels clear and modern first, then quietly human and trustworthy on second look.

### Variant B — Jesus Red

Use for more theological, reflective, essay-like, or journal-style surfaces.

**Direction**

- Parchment and cream backgrounds
- Deep red primary accent
- Warm gold secondary note
- Serif headlines are mandatory
- Paper-grain, journal-like, contemplative atmosphere

**Feels like**

A thoughtful print essay, a leather journal, or a warm theological reflection rather than a tech product page.

### Variant C — Emerald University

Use for academic, institutional, church-leader, or premium editorial contexts where credibility and polish matter more than warmth and simplicity. This is not the default Zoe brand.

**Direction**

- Pure white and off-white canvases
- Emerald primary accents
- Photography-forward, especially campus, library, or study imagery
- Elegant serif headlines with clean sans body copy

**Feels like**

A premium university journal, leadership institute, or polished academic campaign site.

## 12. Page-Family Notes

### Journey lesson pages

Journey lesson pages are their own controlled page family. Use the implementation at `app/journeys/lesson-preview/page.tsx` and `components/JourneyLessonPage.tsx` as the canonical reference, and follow the visual rules documented in `DESIGN.md`.

Key reminders:

- Scenic hero with overlapping lesson card
- Editorial rotated cards for lesson content
- Visible question-mark prompt badge
- Calm field-note comment styling
- Background `#fafcfb`, primary ink `#1f2937`, body copy `#374151`, accent jade `#00c292`, hover jade `#00ae84`, section gold `#d97706`, prayer block `#0f1f1a`
- No scrapbook, sparkle, or generic SaaS treatments
- When refreshing this family, move it closer to the canonical oat + jade + Inter-first Zoe system rather than farther into editorial styling

### Trust & privacy surfaces

Trust design should feel clear and calm. Avoid security-fear aesthetics. Use clean cards, quiet proof points, and concrete language instead of abstract reassurance.

## 13. Non-Negotiables

- Do not make Zoe feel like generic SaaS
- Do not let AI aesthetics overpower spiritual clarity
- Do not use loud or juvenile visual motifs on serious content
- Do not make trust language vague
- Do not sacrifice warmth for polish
- Do not sacrifice legibility for mood

## 14. Working Brand Sentence

When in doubt, return to this:

**Zoe is a quiet companion that helps people walk with Jesus, remember what matters, and stay attentive to God throughout the day.**
